The whole process counts. People included.

Teams and public administrations plan processes around tasks, responsibilities and a clear result. A workflow built entirely around AI can overlook human questions and decisions. Managing human tasks alone does not automatically connect technical steps and their costs either. A shared workflow brings work, handoffs, review, approval and rework together.

Tools such as n8n and Make connect applications and can include human approvals. A complete working process still requires deliberate design of human tasks, responsibilities, feedback and handoffs. c9n starts with building that shared process: What does an agent handle, what does a person work on, and who takes responsibility for the result?

What does a task really cost? Human time spent on work, review and rework × internal hourly rate + AI, infrastructure and allocated tool costs. Total turnaround time, including waiting, and result quality are considered separately. This provides a way to compare a manual workflow with a shared human-and-AI workflow for the same task.

LOCAL VERSION · ORIENTATION

How the local version is organized.

Build processes in Workflows and open the agents involved. The Agents area brings their jobs and controls together. The GPU pool organizes computing capacity for their models. Settings is where you configure connections to other services. This overview explains the intended structure of a local c9n installation.

AgentsGPU poolSettings → Connections
01

Agents

From a shared process to an individual task: the process definition in Workflows links to the agents involved. People review results and give the required approvals.

Overview
See actors, their responsibilities and the current work status together.
Processes
Define a process in Workflows, review its actors and jobs, and open an agent to define its steps. Assign human approval steps to a responsible person within the process.
Jobs
Follow waiting, running and completed tasks. A job belongs to the agent carrying it out and to the relevant process.
Agent details and history
Understand an agent’s assignment, goals and limits, and review available results, feedback and recent runs.
Controls and schedules
Define when an agent should work and check its reported status. A schedule does not replace a required human approval.
02

GPU pool

Provide models and computing capacity to suit each task: on your own computers, on your own network or through external providers you choose to connect.

Pools and utilization
Group available model servers and review their reported capacity and utilization.
Own GPU/model servers
Connect your own computers and servers for AI processing in your environment.
External GPU/model providers
Connect additional computing capacity or models when needed. Required content may then be processed outside your installation.
Queue
See which model calls are waiting for available capacity. This technical queue complements the process job view.
Model and capacity limits
Limit permitted models and available capacity, such as the number of model calls processed at the same time.
Recent calls
Review recently reported model calls and their available run data.
03

Settings → Connections

Configure external services deliberately. Which connection a process uses and which data it needs are part of setting up that process.

Jira · optional
A Jira connection is planned under Settings → Connections to link c9n jobs with Jira issues when needed. Jira is not required for local processes. This does not promise automatic synchronization; connecting Jira exchanges the required data with the configured Jira instance.

How they fit together: A process defines the workflow. A job is the actual work within it. The GPU pool provides model capacity. People are responsible for the required approvals.

Development status: The new menu structure, process execution and integrations are being built. See the release notes for the published scope of each version. The Architecture & Privacy page explains how local processing and external connections work together.
